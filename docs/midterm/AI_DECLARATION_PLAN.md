# AI Declaration Plan

**PLANNING — NOT IMPLEMENTED.** [STATUS.md](STATUS.md) is the midterm tracker.
Declared AI use is allowed; undeclared use is an academic-integrity issue.
Every team member must eventually complete and sign the instructor's actual AI
Declaration. This document is a strategy, not a signed declaration, historical
log, completed evidence entry, or invented commit mapping.

## 1. Recommended declared use level

Recommend **Generated AI use** for the existing heavy workflow and substantive
AI-generated planning, application code, tests or documentation. Human review,
editing, testing, or understanding does not convert generated work into merely
Assisted. Distinguish accepted generated output from advice-only episodes
truthfully, and map the recommendation to the exact instructor form terminology
once the form is supplied. Do not invent a percentage/use level or tick a box on
someone else's behalf.

Each member discloses their own actual use and involvement, including where they
reviewed/explained team code generated by others. The team coordinator maintains
a shared evidence index; individual declarations cannot be replaced by one team
signature. A member who did not use a tool personally must not falsely claim they
did; disclose team-generated components and actual personal review accurately
according to the form. No fake names, initials, signatures or dates now.

## 2. Log unit and required columns

Use one **meaningful development episode** per coherent planning,
implementation, testing/debugging, or documentation task. An episode may group
related prompts in the same conversation/session only if its date range,
components, outputs, decisions and evidence are represented accurately. Split
when purpose, tool/model, outcome or substantially affected component changes;
do not compress materially different work into one vague row or make fake
one-row-per-micro-prompt noise. If the form explicitly requires prompt-level rows,
follow that requirement and use episode IDs as grouping, not as an exemption.

Required columns for the future log (no fabricated entries provided here):

| Column | What must be recorded |
| --- | --- |
| Tool and version | Actual tool/client and available version plus actual displayed model; distinguish Codex app/CLI version from model identity. Do not infer actual model from the requested label. |
| Date | Real episode date or accurate start/end dates, timezone where useful; use visible capture/session timestamps. Never invent dates to fill gaps. |
| Component/file affected | Specific real paths/functions; planning affects only the five `docs/midterm` files. |
| Purpose | Concrete reason for using AI. |
| Prompt summary | Faithful concise description of requests and constraints; retain full linked/screenshot evidence. |
| What AI produced | Actual design/code/test/documentation/advice; identify what entered the work. |
| Accepted / modified / rejected | Actual disposition and significant human changes/rejection reasons; mixed outcomes may be recorded explicitly. |
| Commit hash | Actual resulting reviewed commit(s), or honestly `UNCOMMITTED` / `NO COMMIT` with reason until one exists. |

Helpful extra fields: episode ID, actual responsible member/reviewer, evidence
paths/export links, verification references. These supplement required columns;
they do not replace them. An actual planning episode may later be logged from
this conversation with its real date and five files; map it to the actual
planning SHA only after this authorized checkpoint exists. Do not prefill a fake sample row.

## 3. Truthful history reconstruction

The baseline has a heavy AI-assisted/generated development history. Reconstruct
meaningful episodes from real retained chats/exports/screenshots and Git diffs,
with the people involved verifying dates, files, outcomes and use level. Do not
attribute every past prompt to a member or checkpoint from memory alone.

If evidence is unavailable, record the limitation and actual known facts in the
final disclosure; ask the instructor how to handle unavailable historical proof.
Do not fabricate replacement dated screenshots, chats, accepted/rejected
decisions, model versions, or commit correspondences. Historical feature SHAs in
repository docs can guide inspection, but they are not automatic proof of which
AI episode produced the code. Current captures are current captures, not past
development evidence.

## 4. Evidence organization under `/ai-evidence`

Future root folder `/ai-evidence` is the instructor-specified repository-relative
organization. No folder, screenshot, log, export or evidence manifest is created
in this task. Once separately approved, organize by real episode/type:

```text
ai-evidence/
  index.md                     # reviewed episode-to-evidence/link index
  planning/<actual-episode>/   # real dated captures/exports
  implementation/<actual-episode>/
  testing-debugging/<actual-episode>/
  documentation/<actual-episode>/
```

Use exported chat links or **full screenshots with visible date**, as required.
A summary alone or tightly cropped undated snippet is insufficient proof. Keep
complete original evidence; verify exported links resolve with instructor access
and show the required content/date. Use actual capture metadata/capture dates
and real episode IDs in filenames, never a fictional historical timestamp.

Preserve originals locally outside Git. Before any later Git addition, explicitly
review each file/link for safe sharing, readable dates, accessible scope and
truthful output/commit association. A full dated screenshot can still reveal
secrets elsewhere on screen; inspect it, never publish credentials to satisfy
proof. If a full required capture contains sensitive material, retain the original
privately and arrange instructor-approved private evidence/redaction handling;
label any redacted copy accurately rather than passing it as unedited proof.
Do not silently redact/crop away the evidence requirement or create fake output.

This task permits only the five planning documents. Even safe real evidence
requires later scope/authorization before it is added or uploaded.

## 5. Actual commit mapping strategy

1. At episode end, identify actual resulting paths/diffs and mark uncommitted
   work as **UNCOMMITTED** until committed. Obtain this planning checkpoint SHA
   from the verified execution report; do not invent or self-reference a hash.
2. After explicit checkpoint authorization, verify the created SHA and inspected
   diff; replace the placeholder with that **actual** SHA and relevant files.
   A documentation-only episode must not claim authorship of pre-existing R1/R2
   feature commits just because it discussed them.
3. An episode can map to multiple real commits; a commit can reference multiple
   actual episodes. Explain the relationship, especially mixed accepted/modified
   output. For entirely rejected/advice-only output, use **NO COMMIT** with reason
   unless a genuine document/result was committed.
4. Keep original source checkpoint references when later cherry-picks/merges create
   new SHAs; add real reconciliation links after verifying them. Never invent
   future hashes or rewrite historical author/date records for member credit.
5. Final audit compares log rows, evidence dates, affected paths, actual Git diffs,
   verification results and member contributions. Resolve mismatches truthfully
   before submission; unknown mappings remain stated gaps.

## 6. Verification and understanding statements

Every member writes their own specific verification statement **after doing the
work**, including what they reviewed/tested, actual commands/results or browser
behaviors, accepted/modified/rejected output, and remaining limitations. “All AI
output verified” is inappropriate if tests were skipped or other components were
not reviewed. Cite actual evidence/checkpoints rather than generic assurances.

At planning stage, the eventual statement should report documentation/source
inspection and scope/diff checks only; no API call, DB query/write, application
test, build, migration or runtime acceptance happened here. Later implementation
statements distinguish source tests from real browser/DB acceptance and identify
their synthetic target explicitly.

Each member's understanding statement follows a real file/function walkthrough:
explain inputs/outputs, session/role checks, Zod/bound SQL/transaction behavior or
relevant UI behavior, failure cases, and why the member's genuine change works.
Record uncertainties and resolve them through learning/review; do not sign an
AI-written blanket claim of understanding without demonstration. Cross-reference
the member's real change/verification/YouTube proof from the evidence plan.

## 7. Privacy and signature handling

Never put plaintext passwords, credential-bearing env files, DB connection
secrets, raw session tokens/cookies, real hashes, unnecessary personal data or
private account details in Git, public video, prompts, exported share links or
screenshots. Synthetic hash evidence is handled privately as specified in the
evidence plan. Include only required complete member names in final submission;
avoid extra IDs/contact details/signature scans in repository history.

Every member personally completes and signs the actual declaration with the
real signing date after reviewing their evidence. Keep handwritten/digital
signatures and signed forms in the private final submission workspace, **outside
Git**, unless the instructor explicitly requires them in Git. Confirm form and
signature method first; no fabricated signature or typed “signed” placeholder.

| Material | Git policy / final submission handling |
| --- | --- |
| These five unsigned planning docs | Authorized now for explicit five-file staging, one planning commit, and dedicated midterm branch push. |
| Truthful unsigned AI log/evidence index and shareable captures/links | May be added under `/ai-evidence` later after review, scope and checkpoint authorization; preserve required dated full proof. |
| Signed individual forms / private signature originals | Private final submission only by default; no Git/public upload unless instructor explicitly requires it. |
| Sensitive full original chats/screenshots | Local/private instructor channel if required; safe reviewed copies/links only with accurate labels and acceptance. |
| Credentials, raw session data, real personal records/hashes | Excluded; never submitted as ordinary development proof. |
